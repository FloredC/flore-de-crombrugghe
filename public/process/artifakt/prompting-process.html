Artifakt — Image Generation & Prompting Process

Documents the technical pipeline, per-artist prompting strategy, and design decisions for the AI-powered sketch-to-artwork transformation. Last updated: June 2026.

01 How the pipeline works

Artifakt uses a two-pass image-to-image pipeline. The user draws a sketch; that sketch is preprocessed per artist, then sent through two sequential API calls to fal.ai's Flux Dev model. Pass 1 establishes gesture and material quality. Pass 2 applies the artist's full visual identity on top.

Why two passes?

Pass 1 establishes material quality (how the drawing is physically made) without contaminating it with the artist's name. Pass 2 then adds color, motifs and full style identity on top of a structurally sound base.

What strength controls

Strength = how much the model departs from the input image. 0.0 = output identical to input. 1.0 = input ignored entirely. Higher strength = more artistic transformation but looser sketch fidelity.

Why results vary

Diffusion models start from random noise (the seed). Two identical prompts produce different results every time. Variance compounds across two passes. The ↻ regenerate button on the canvas lets users reroll.

Model used

fal-ai/flux/dev/image-to-image. 28 inference steps, guidance scale 3.5. No LoRA fine-tuning — style is driven entirely through prompting. LoRA is a future option once the artist roster is validated.

Scaffold (Screen 1)

The guide drawing is generated via fal-ai/flux/schnell (text-to-image, 4 steps). Prompt steers toward feminine/androgynous figures when keyword involves a person, to avoid default male representations.

Cost

Two API calls per artist per generation. All 5 artists are pre-generated in parallel when Screen 2 loads. Regenerating one artist = 2 fresh calls for that artist only.

02 Artist roster & rationale

Artists were chosen to be female, queer or trans — reflecting the feminist values of the project. Each artist needed to be recognizable to the Flux model (sufficient training data) and visually distinct from the others through both linework and color palette. Abstract artists (Kusama, Mehretu) were removed because their visual language doesn't transfer well through img2img prompting without LoRA training.

03 Current prompts per artist

Each artist has two prompts (Pass 1 and Pass 2) and a sketch preprocessing config. Pass 1 describes how the drawing is physically made — the gesture and material. Pass 2 adds the artist's name, color palette, and decorative elements. The artist name only appears in Pass 2 to avoid contaminating the material layer.

04 Strength values at a glance

Strength controls how much the model transforms the input. Higher = more artistic freedom, looser sketch fidelity. Each artist has a different sweet spot depending on how radical their visual language is.

Artist Pass 1 strength Pass 2 strength Rationale
Louise Bourgeois 0.82 0.85 Needs high freedom in both passes to build strand texture and dominant red color
Kara Walker 0.78 0.70 Lower P2 to protect the silhouette built by preprocessing; just adds narrative figures
Niki de Saint Phalle 0.80 0.88 High P2 needed to flood every shape with saturated flat color
Naoko Takeuchi 0.75 0.80 Lower P1 to keep delicate lines; P2 adds manga eyes and pastel washes
Keith Haring 0.80 0.90 Highest P2 of all — neon color fills need maximum transformation freedom
05 Key decisions log

A record of the major technical and creative decisions made during development, and the reasoning behind each.

Decision
Session 4–5
What
Replaced Kusama and Mehretu with Walker, Saint Phalle, Takeuchi, Haring
Why
Abstract painters don't transfer well through img2img prompting — Flux doesn't have enough training data on them, and abstract styles require LoRA fine-tuning to work. Replaced with artists whose visual language is figurative enough to be recognizable.
Decision
Session 5
What
Added per-artist sketch preprocessing (ARTIST_SKETCH_CONFIG)
Why
Different artists need different input qualities. Walker needs thick lines (14px) + flood fill to create a solid silhouette. Haring needs bold lines (9px). Takeuchi needs thin delicate lines (4px). The preprocessing does structural work the prompt alone can't do.
Decision
Session 5
What
Moved to two-pass pipeline instead of single pass
Why
Single pass at moderate strength produced 5 nearly identical clean outline drawings. Separating gesture+material (Pass 1, no artist name) from finish (Pass 2, artist name + color) allows each layer to do its job cleanly without interference.
Decision
Session 5
What
Artist name only appears in Pass 2, not Pass 1
Why
Including the artist name in Pass 1 contaminates the material layer — the model starts applying style before the structural quality is established. Pass 1 prompt describes the physical drawing tool and mark-making, Pass 2 prompt adds the artist's name and visual identity.
Decision
Session 5
What
Reverted from flux/schnell back to flux/dev for img2img
Why
flux/schnell (4-step distilled model) produced wildly hallucinated results for stylized artwork — e.g. entire canvas covered in random red polka dots. flux/dev with 28 steps + guidance_scale 3.5 gives the model enough structure to follow the prompt accurately.
Decision
Session 6
What
Rewrote Bourgeois prompts around strand-like parallel strokes, not contour lines
Why
Reference images showed that Bourgeois builds forms through accumulated parallel strands (like hair, fiber, roots) — not through repeated contour lines. The original prompt was describing the wrong construction method. The new prompt describes volume through repetition and directionality.
Decision
Session 6
What
Added scaffold prompt instruction to avoid default male figures
Why
When keywords like "yogi" or "dancer" were used, the scaffold generated typically male figures. Added instruction: "if depicting a person or body: feminine or androgynous or non-binary figure, queer aesthetic" to align the guide image with the feminist values of the project.
Decision
Session 6
What
Added ↻ regenerate button directly on the canvas (not in action bar)
Why
Diffusion model variance is permanent — different seeds always produce different results. Instead of fighting this, the UX embraces it. The button lives on the image (top-right corner), appears only after generation completes, regenerates only the current artist, clears just that artist's cache.
06 Open questions & next steps
Topic Status Notes
Louise Bourgeois results Needs work The strand-parallel-stroke quality is still hard to achieve through prompting alone. LoRA training on Bourgeois reference images may be needed to capture this specific mark-making language.
Walker flood fill bug Known bug Flood fill from canvas center fails for mechanical/open shapes (bicycle, etc.) — fills entire black background instead of subject. Needs a smarter flood fill approach or a fallback for non-figurative subjects.
Loading time In progress Two passes of flux/dev = ~30–40s per artist. flux/schnell was tested but too unpredictable for stylized artwork. Consider improved loading animations or showing Pass 1 result as a preview while Pass 2 loads.
LoRA fine-tuning Future option $2/artist/run on fal.ai. Needs 4+ reference images per artist. Would close the remaining quality gap, especially for Bourgeois. Recommended after artist roster is validated through user testing.
Fidelity slider Future feature "Stay close to my sketch" ↔ "Be more artistic" — maps to strength parameter. Would give users direct control over the sketch fidelity vs transformation tradeoff.
Upload Takeuchi + Haring references to Cloudinary Pending ARTIST_REFERENCES has empty strings for Takeuchi and Haring. Currently unused (model doesn't support style_image param), but needed if LoRA path is taken.
