# Voice principles: Flore

How Flore writes when she is not performing. Applies to case studies, LinkedIn
posts, emails and professional communication. Illustration captions come later,
in their own section.

Every rule below has a line of hers as proof. If a draft breaks a rule, flag
it. Do not rewrite her into a smoother version.

Lines marked [confirm] are not yet confirmed by Flore.

## Using this in this repo

The copy this governs lives in `src/content/` — `.mdx` per project, `.js` per
case study. To read a case study's prose without the surrounding module, run
`npm run copy <slug>` and read `copy/<slug>.md`; see `scripts/copy.mjs`.

that a session working on the copy has it without being handed it. A second

## Rules

**1. She opens with context: a place, a date, a person. Never a thesis.**
> "Last December we celebrated our company's 10 year anniversary in our studio in Zurich."
> "I've been walking around my borough this week and was drawn to a vintage storefront on Freihofstrasse I didn't know about."

**2. She closes by handing the turn to the reader, or on a quiet aftereffect. Never a moral, never a pitch.**
> "N'hésitez pas à me contacter si vous passez par Zürich."
> "I haven't stopped thinking about her since."

**3. Claude never adds a punch line. No one-line takeaways, no "not X, but Y" reframes, no slogans invented in the edit. Slogans Flore wrote herself stay unless she cuts them.**
> "I'm afraid of sounding like a bunch of AI punch lines."
> Kept by her in the Artifakt page: "People want to have made it, and they want it to look good. They don't want to choose."

**4. The insight stays attached to what happened. She admits partial failure plainly. With a failure comes a learning. Doubt is stated once, as a plain sentence, never as a note to self.**
> "I could partly transfer these principles I learned at school but not fully, as I couldn't control which images the pipeline used as a reference."
> Added by her in the Artifakt page: "This works most of the time, though not perfectly."

**5. Self-initiated projects start with what she wanted to learn, then the research question, then what it taught her about people. Never a retrofitted user need. Thoughts lead with a lot of questions, which are important hooks to the specific standpoint or angle the project gets.**
> "I make these products because I want to learn about AI, and to "not give up" in the process, I need to do something I feel excited about."
> "What if an Ai image generation tool could inspire and teach people about artists instead of making something that looks like AI and doesn't feel authentic? What does authenticity mean exactly when sharing a personalized, visual intention. Where does the threshold lie between something that feels self made, personal and a bit imperfect and an image that is clearly generic and created by a machine?"

**6. When cutting, remove repetition and atmosphere. Keep the origin of an idea, its honest limit, and personal details that explain a decision or that answer a question.**
> "I really want to try to cut my stories to the essentials so that important things I want to say are transeferred."
> "I produced this cover, a first version of this version C, in the train from Zurich to Berlin."

**7. Sentences are mostly short to medium. She breaks rhythm by subtraction: a sentence, then a fragment.**
> "Something about her was kind and soft. Comforting. Like the smell of a hot, fresh mint tea on a Sunday coffee table."
> "That's when something happened."

**8. Em dashes only exceptionally, when a comma, period, colon or conjunction cannot do the job.**
> "its not a hard no but it should be used only exceptionally."

**9. When she disagrees: concede the cost, name the risk, state the position, ask. She lets the other side's words speak and turns a lost argument into her own lesson.**
> "So they proposed to use 222 words instead of 333, which we refused because the concept of our book was actually to step out of the binary system."
> "Das würde natürlich die Seitenzahl erweitern, aber ohne die Quellen befinden wir uns auf sehr dünnem Eis. Wir wären daher für eine Quellenliste mit ganz kleiner Typo. Was denkt ihr hierzu?"
> "So the learning from this is that next time I will put much more attention and effort in the cover from the start"

**10. She corrects herself in the open and holds words at arm's length with quotation marks.**
> "I find myself prompting, or should I call it writing, lately."

**11. Plain words. "really", "actually", "playful", "cumbersome". Exclamation marks for warmth, never for claims.**
> "They've really helped me work with AI in a more structured way within the UX design process"
> "I'm so happy to meet you all!"

**12. She writes for design peers, who can also be design managers. Don't explain craft basics. Never overclaim.**
> "design peers can be design managers"

**13. References are woven into the thought, credited, with honest uncertainty. The quote opens a question about her own work.** [confirm]
> "I remember John Maeda writing somewhere (can't find the source) that writing code is like writing a novel"

**14. Traces of her other languages stay when deliberate. Fix real slips. Flag the borderline ones as a question. Stricter with strangers, looser in posts. If foreign words are used, they should be flagged clearly so that I can decide whether to keep them or not.**
> "reduce the hurdle of "drawing something" for someone else"
> "The ability to truly malax it"

**15. Wording has to be immediately clear to a design peer. If she wouldn't say it out loud, it goes. Compressed or abstract phrasing is wrong even when the point underneath is right.**
> "the ai writes about the concern I want to write about but not in a way I would say it"

Flagged by Flore in the live Artifakt page:

| Line | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "The defaults are thin, white and European, and they hold until you actively displace them." | The second half of the sentence is hard to understand. |
| "The scaffold was too complete, making tracing feel like busywork, not creation." | "busywork" is not her word. She would say the tracing felt pointless. |
| "Works: explicit attribute language — skin tone, hair, body type — plus foreground anchoring and a rotating subject pool so one default can't dominate." | Everything after "plus" is hard to understand. |

Word swaps she made in the Artifakt page:

| Claude wrote | Flore writes |
|---|---|
| a scaffold to trace | an example to trace |
| the wobble in a traced line | the traced line |
| Fix 1, Fix 2 | Solution 1, Solution 2 |
| Wanted / Got / Dead ends | Wished result / The result I got / What didn't work |

The fix is never a synonym. Say the same thing in the words she would use out
loud, even if it takes one sentence more.

**16. Form is hers, voice is the job. Flag wording and content. Do not flag bold, labels, headings or layout: she uses those for scanning and decides them herself.**
> In the Artifakt review she kept the bold passages and the Lesson labels, and cut the slogan headers herself.

## HARD NO

Words she never uses. This list grows: add one whenever it annoys her.
- gamechanger

Also absent from everything she wrote herself:
- leverage, passionate, seamless, empower, delve, thrilled
- "at the intersection of", "through-line", "I'd argue", "extensive experience"
- "not X, but Y" reframes
- one-line takeaways and slogans ("reframe before you tune")
- arrows or bold numbers as the conclusion
- closing with a pitch ("Happy to show you what I've been building.")
- opening with a big statement about the industry

She must never sound like a salesperson.
