Case-study page — the block system, built on PitchPivot

The subpage system for /work/:slug: a width-based block vocabulary, its spacing rhythm, and the copy and research data that fill it. PitchPivot is the reference implementation. Artifakt is the second page built on it — and the session that found the vocabulary is worth sharing while the composition is not.

17 Aug 2026 Copy, research integrity, spacing and assets Building with Claude

A long pass over the PitchPivot page, which had been built the previous session but never read end to end. What started as a copy edit turned into a data-integrity audit — several numbers on the page turned out to be unsourced, and one claim about the research participants was invented outright. The session ended on the spacing system and the asset pipeline.

The research data — five real errors found

The charts and figures had been carried over from an earlier build with no link back to source. Opening the two survey PDFs (a pypdf install in a scratch venv — the machine had no PDF text extractor) and the two Mentimeter results pages settled every number. Worth doing early next time: it took ten minutes and found five things.

Was on the pageActuallyWhy it mattered
All three charts labelled n=24 Two different surveys: opening (28 respondents, 27 answered most questions) and closing (24) "Blockers to design influence" is an opening-survey question, so its base was 27, not 24
Barriers chart against 24 participants Asked only of the 20 who said "maybe" or "no" Every bar was reading against the wrong base — 9 of 20 is 45%, not 9 of 24
"multiple answers allowed" Mentimeter question is literally "Pick your 2 biggest blockers" Changes what the top bar's 17 means
"whether they would use it again" "Would you use PitchPivot before your next stakeholder buy-in?" "Again" implies prior adoption; the real question is forward intent
"Sixty-three per cent said maybe" Yes 4 / Maybe 15 / No 5 A percentage on 24 people claims precision the sample can't carry. The full split — "only four said yes" — is also the stronger finding
The one that stings. The page claimed the interviews ran "from individual contributors to design leads." That was derived from the three quote attributions below it — one of which read "Design lead, SaaS." Those attributions were unedited Figma placeholder text. Flore interviewed no design leads. So design-file copy was treated as research data, and a false claim about real participants ended up on a portfolio page. Rule for next time: nothing in a Figma instance is evidence. Only the survey exports and Flore's own words are.

Decisions

Takeaways
One theme per takeaway — 01 is workflow, 02 is output quality, and neither borrows the other's material
Both had accreted paragraphs until each carried both themes: the phone and the "too generic" finding appeared in both. Cutting the overlap took the three takeaways from 155/180/99 words to 67/62/72 — evenly weighted, and each one now readable on its own.
Takeaway 01
Name the scope constraint as a chosen tradeoff, not a finding
"Users said it should fit the workflow, so that's next" reads as learning something obvious late. Flore knew workflow integration was stronger before building; standalone was a five-week scope decision. Saying so is the difference between showing judgement and admitting an oversight.
Takeaway 02
Refuse the excuse about testing conditions
A draft argued the 5.5 output score was measured unfairly (everyone tested on a phone, in six minutes). Flore flagged it as defensive — and it was: an untested hypothesis used as mitigation, with no desktop data behind it. It also contradicted takeaway 01 directly above, which owns a tradeoff. The paragraph was eventually cut entirely: it raised a caveat and then dismissed its own caveat.
Voice
First person belongs to the Guide speech bubbles only
The Turning Point had a first-person paragraph and a first-person bubble. The fix wasn't to write about assumptions in third person — it was to move the finding into the prose (naturally third person) and leave the bubble the one thing only an aside can do: "I went in convinced this was a presentation problem. It wasn't."
Voice
No roadmap language anywhere
Flore isn't continuing the product. "The next version has to aim…" promised work that isn't happening. Reframing to what such a tool would need made takeaway 03 stronger, not weaker — it now ends "arguably not in a tool at all," which is a bolder conclusion than a plan.
Copy
Substance, not vocabulary: "answer real business needs", not "in business terms"
Flore's correction, and it's the sharpest distinction in the page. "Business terms" is a translation problem — say it in different words. The real claim is that the decision itself has to answer a business need. An intermediate draft ("pivot how they frame it for whoever is in the room") drifted the same way from the other side, into audience-adaptation.
Copy
29 em dashes → 14
Flore asked why so many. Honest answer: habit, not 29 decisions. An em dash earns its place on a sharp reversal or an aside with internal commas; everywhere else a comma or full stop is quieter. Overused, none of them land — and it reads as machine-written, which is the last thing a portfolio page in her voice needs.
Spacing
Add a "within a chapter" step: 40 inside, 140 between
The scale had tight 12, default 24 and break 80/120 — but every block was a flat sibling at break, so a section header sat exactly as far from its own prose as from the next chapter. There was no distance meaning "these belong together," which is exactly why the chapters were hard to grasp. Both numbers measured off the Figma frame.
Layout
Onward drops to medium so the momentum figure can be full width
Rule 1 (no two consecutive blocks share a width) allows only one of them to be wide. The momentum panel is the page's largest evidence; Onward is a 562px card and a CTA that never filled 1184.
Emphasis
**bold** markers in content strings, rendered as <strong>
Content stays plain strings — countable, diffable, movable between fields. A markdown library would be a dependency and a much larger surface for one construct. Eleven phrases marked, one per paragraph, on the claim rather than the evidence: read the bold alone and you get the argument.

What didn't survive contact with the build

Alternating feature rows

The build spec asked for media alternating left/right. Figma draws both rows with text at x=0. Figma was right — flagged as a conflict for weeks, settled in one sentence.

Button below the hero media

An earlier pass moved "Try it out" below the image, reasoning that overlapping could cover artwork. Flore's mock put it back on the edge — and the risk didn't apply, since the button is centred and the only content along that edge is left-aligned.

"You are here" on subpages

Reusing the homepage breadcrumb seemed like good component hygiene. It reads out of context on a standalone page: it points at a map the reader can't see.

Rule 1 as a free constraint

The no-two-consecutive-widths rule forced three separate compromises this session (Guide alignment twice, curve width once). It's still worth keeping, but it isn't free — every new block negotiates with its neighbours.

Technical traps worth remembering

Vite doesn't fingerprint public/ assets. Re-exported PNGs keep the same URL, so the browser serves the old bytes. This cost real back-and-forth — twice, an asset that had been correctly replaced on disk looked "not replaced" in the browser. Hard-reload after every asset swap, and verify assets by reading the pixels, not the file timestamp.
The ch unit resolves against the element it's written on. The speech bubble's max-w-[38ch] sat on a wrapper that inherited the body's 16px while the text rendered at 18px, so every measure came out ~10% narrow — and looked completely correct in the code. Only visible by measuring in the browser. Also: HK Grotesk's 0 is ~0.56em, not the 0.63em an earlier note in the repo assumed.
Prettier without the project's config rewrote a file to double quotes and semicolons, inconsistent with the rest of the codebase — and silently broke a scripted edit that was matching on single quotes. Run it with --single-quote --no-semi or not at all.
Baked chrome in exports fights CSS chrome. The hero asset shipped with a light-blue outline and 14px-rounded corners drawn into the PNG, while the frame applies its own border and a 24px radius — so the grid showed through as a crescent at each corner. Resolved 2026-08-19: Flore confirms the outline is intentional and the result is not a problem. This stands as something to be aware of when composing an export against a CSS frame, not as a defect to fix. It was raised as an open task several times before she was asked — the lesson is to check whether an apparent artefact is a choice.

Figma → Claude working notes

Meta-knowledge, and the most reusable part of this session.

  • The Figma MCP works from this repo. It had been assumed to need authorisation; it doesn't. Sampling nodes directly settled several questions in seconds that would otherwise have been guesses — Desktop/body is 18px/1.5, bubble padding is 14 all round, section spacing is 40 within and ~140 between.
  • Node geometry is more reliable than any screenshot. The spacing system came from reading y-positions and frame heights, not from eyeballing the frame. Text at y=40 in a 704-tall section, next section starting 40 later — that's a rhythm you can implement exactly.
  • Name instances with real content, and edit the placeholder copy. The one genuinely damaging error this session came from an unedited component default being read as data.
  • Export composite graphics as parts. Splitting the momentum curve from its tool legend is what let them sit side by side on desktop and stack on mobile. A single flattened export can only ever have one arrangement.
  • Give Claude the raw research files early. The PDFs and Mentimeter links resolved every open number and found five errors. Ten minutes, at the start, not the end.

Still open

  • The hero asset still needs re-exporting square and unstroked. Closed 2026-08-19 — intentional, per Flore. Not an issue.
  • Whether 140 feels like a chapter break — the numbers match Figma, the judgement is Flore's, deferred to tomorrow.
  • Takeaway 01 carries two bolded phrases, the densest on the page. If it reads heavy, "None of that was a surprise." is the one to cut.
  • The other five case studies haven't been migrated onto this system — PitchPivot is still the only page using it, deliberately.
  • A display type step (48px) is still missing; the hero title renders at h1 (36) as the nearest token.

21–25 Aug 2026 Artifakt — the system's second page, which didn't use the system Building with Claude

Building the Artifakt case study on the block vocabulary above — except it turned out not to fit, and forcing it would have been the wrong call. The session ran across several days of design-and-build ping-pong: Flore edited the Figma frame while the page was being built, and the copy was re-pulled four separate times. It ended with the process logs published as real pages, a diagram rebuilt from an iframe into a component, and 42 MB of HTML turned into 0.2 MB.

The central decision — one system, two compositions

The doc above argues for a width-based block vocabulary with PitchPivot as the reference implementation, and says the other five case studies should inherit it. The first attempt to do that failed, and it's worth being precise about why, because the conclusion isn't "the system was wrong".

21 Aug
Give Artifakt its own composition file rather than routing it through CaseStudy.jsx
CaseStudy.jsx reads named slots — what, turningPoint, features, takeaways, process — and renders a fixed sequence of typed evidence components. Artifakt has none of those shapes: no StatGrid, no three QuoteCards, no RankedBars, no FeatureBlock pair. Forcing it through would have meant naming its "How I worked" section turningPoint to satisfy a key. That's a lie in the data model that every future reader has to decode.
21 Aug
Share the visual system, not the composition
Flore's clarification settled it: "I was talking more about the visual structure and the spaces." Frame, SectionHeader, Media, Container, ButtonLink, ProjectCard and the spacing tokens are all imported. Only the section order and shapes differ — which is exactly the part that genuinely differs in the design file. Concrete payoff: nothing in the new page can regress PitchPivot, and a spacing fix in a shared primitive still reaches both.
21 Aug
Scope "Rule 1" to PitchPivot instead of bending Artifakt to satisfy it
Rule 1 — no two consecutive blocks share a width — throws in dev. Artifakt's frame gets its rhythm the opposite way: every text container sits at a fixed 720 measure all the way down, and the media does the pacing (1282 full stages, a 720 stage, 523 side-by-sides, one radius-60 showcase). Applying Rule 1 would have fired on the second section and forced a rhythm the design doesn't have.
25 Aug
Chapter spacing overridden in code, not sampled from the frame
The frame draws 40 of padding either side of a section, giving a flat 80 between chapters. Flore asked for PitchPivot's rhythm instead — SPACE.break, 80 at small sizes and 140 from xl. Pointed at the shared constant rather than hardcoding 140, so the two pages can't drift. Noted in the file because anyone re-sampling the frame later will find 80 and think the code is wrong.

Four bugs — and what each one says about where to look

The pattern worth carrying forward: three of these four survived a clean npm run build. The bundler is not a correctness check. Every one was found by opening the page and measuring it.

BugSymptomCause
Popover state racereported by Flore Two of five pipeline steps didn't open on hover — "or only after a first click" Moving between neighbours fires two callbacks: open on the one entered, close on the one left. Order isn't guaranteed, React batches both, last writer wins. A close landing second overwrote the open. Fixed by scoping the close: a step may only close itself.
Iframe src not base-prefixed Embed silently collapsed to 15px in production; dev looked perfect A content-supplied path Vite never sees, so it resolved against the domain root. Fixed by moving assetUrl inside the component so no call site can forget — the homepage had remembered, the new call site hadn't.
Deleted constants Page rendered completely blank A scripted block replacement cut three const declarations along with the function it was rewriting. ✓ built in 1.28s, then MEDIA_LAYOUT is not defined at runtime.
Missing box-sizing Fifth pipeline step wrapped onto its own row and stretched to full width 190px min-width + 28px padding + 2px border = 220px actual under content-box. Five of those overflowed a 1114 row by 110px. Read as "a different kind of object", not as a layout bug.
The self-inflicted one. The image-externalisation script deleted all 137 extracted images on its second run. A doc already converted was skipped, so it contributed nothing to the "accounted for" set — and --prune then treated every file as an orphan. Recovered from a backup taken minutes earlier. Two fixes: a skipped doc now registers the references it already carries, and the script refuses to prune when that would delete everything in a folder. The second guard is redundant with the first on purpose.

What didn't survive contact with the build

  • The pipeline diagram as an embed. Built as a self-contained HTML iframe, and the hover previews were clipped at the frame's bottom edge. Not fixable inside an embed: an iframe is a hard clipping boundary, the parent sizes it to scrollHeight, and an absolutely-positioned popover contributes nothing to that. Rebuilt in-page, where the popover portals to <body> and flip/shift keep it on screen. Three things fell out of the move that were worth more than the fix: the embed had hardcoded #420bc1 and #8b5cf6 because an iframe can't read its parent's custom properties (they're real tokens); it carried its own copies of three HK Grotesk weights; and it duplicated a reset, a type scale and a colour scale in a second stylesheet nobody would think to update.
  • Uploading images by hand. 143 files uploaded, of which 46 were in none of the six documents — and 40 document images had no file at all. The documents went on ignoring the folder because their src attributes still pointed at inlined base64. Replaced with a script that reads each doc, writes every inlined image out content-hashed, and rewrites the reference. 41.8 MB → 0.2 MB of HTML.
  • Hand-editing the process logs to add navigation. Figma asks for the subpage navbar on the log pages. The obvious move — paste a header into each of the six files — was wrong: they're generated exports, and the repo already learned this with language-river.html. Six hand-edited headers is six edits to redo on every re-export. A route wraps them instead, so re-exporting a log is a pure file drop.
  • A width cap carried across assets. MEDIA_WIDTH.hero is 450, measured from PitchPivot's 1798×2234 hero (ratio 0.80). Artifakt's is 545×1185 (ratio 0.46) — a phone inside browser chrome. At 450 it rendered 976px tall, so the hero alone filled a laptop viewport and the page appeared to start empty. A display width carried to a different aspect ratio is a different height, and it looks untouched in the diff.

Figma → Claude working notes

Continuing the list from the previous session. These are all new, and all cost real time before being understood.

  • Markdown pasted into a text layer is not styled copy. The frame first arrived with - , > and ** rendering as literal characters, so the design showed no bullet, quote or emphasis treatment at all. After Flore restyled it in Figma the same content came back as real lists, real bold/italic runs and italic asides — and only then could the block types be read off it. Restyling in Figma is what makes the copy machine-readable, not just prettier.
  • [BUILT] in a frame name is load-bearing and was on the wrong frames. Per the repo's own convention it means "code wins, don't re-pull this copy" — and it was sitting on two frames whose pages didn't exist yet. Left alone it would have told the next session to ignore the very frame it was meant to build from.
  • Re-read node values every time; they move. The against-the-defaults artwork was 394.79 wide in the morning and 333.5 by the afternoon. Both were "the Figma value". The only reason the cap is right is that it was re-read rather than reused.
  • Layer names lie; only the text content is data. Half the frame's text layers are still named "PitchPivot helps designers translate design thinki" — Figma keeps the name from creation. One caption was flagged as wrong based on a layer name plus an inference from a neighbouring screenshot, and that flag was itself wrong.
  • Duplicated frames carry duplicated content errors. Two thumbnail filenames were crossed relative to what they picture, and a caption from one section had been pasted onto another's stage. Matching by filename would have shipped both. Every thumbnail was opened and matched to what it actually shows.
  • The design file can be behind the code, and knowing which way round matters. Five things ended the session with code ahead of Figma — two Guide bubbles, one paragraph, two white stages and a deleted embed stage. All flagged in the files, because a re-pull would silently undo them.

A parked investigation, with numbers

Flore reported that everything felt too big on a 1500px laptop and asked for a structured plan. The measurement contradicted two of the premises, so the plan was written and then deliberately shelved — the findings are in process-docs/vertical-density/ with a re-runnable harness.

  • The type scale is already fluid. Every step is a clamp() between 402 and 1622. There are no breakpoints to add; the question is whether the desktop anchors are right, which is a different question with a different answer.
  • The constraint is viewport height, and type scales on width. The featured card measures 878px at 1280×800, 883px at 1500×850, 887px at 1728×1080 — nine pixels of variation across a 450px width range. It isn't too big; laptops are short. No width-keyed breakpoint can express that.
  • Type is a weak lever where it was assumed to be strong. A 12% type reduction takes 3.9% off the homepage and 8.0% off a case study. Spacing at −30% gives −8.8%. The featured card is 68% image — 600px of media against 260px of text.
  • "Every chapter in one viewport" isn't reachable by scaling. Two chapters on each case study sit at 2.0–2.4 viewports. Getting there needs −54%; type and spacing together give −10%. Those need structural change, not a smaller type scale.

Narration — what the page was missing

Flore asked for a short evaluation of the narration logic once the page was built. Three of the four findings became work in the same session.

24 Aug
The artists were named once and never shown
"Bring an artist in" is one of three stated goals, and the page argued most readers would meet one of these artists for the first time here — while showing five names in a bullet. Fixed with one traced sketch rendered five ways, placed directly under the goal that claims it. It's also a premise for "Designing against the model's defaults" much later, which only lands once the roster has been seen.
24 Aug
No stakes — every problem on the page was a design problem, not a human one
The page went from "what it is" straight to "the question I faced". A hiring manager arriving cold learned what was built and how well it was solved, but not why it should exist. Split in two: the effort problem opens the establishing section, and "effort only counts if the recipient can see it" opens the reveal, where the wobble in the traced line is the proof.
24 Aug
The Guide became load-bearing narration instead of an aside
A section's prose opened on "Six interviews… sharpened it", where "it" was the question the avatar had just asked. Readers skim past avatars. PitchPivot's contract is that the Guide reacts to prose that already stands alone — restored here, with the prose regaining its opener and the bubble becoming "I went in assuming the barrier was emotional. It wasn't."

Still open

  • Five places where the code is ahead of Figma — two Guide bubbles (scaffold, testing were replaced by Flore; the reveal's opening paragraph is still mine), the white stages on user-testing and final-product, and the second embed stage that should be deleted from the frame.
  • The scaffold image has the same over-sizing the defaults image had — 524 rendered against 401 in the frame. Not raised, so not changed.
  • "The per-artist prompt table" points at the prompting-process log, which is an assumption about where that table lives.
  • Hero.jsx:180 drives the map hotspots with the same unguarded open/close shape that caused the popover race. It rarely fires there because the hotspots are far apart. One-line fix, not applied.
  • Vertical density is parked with a plan and a baseline. The single highest-value piece is the featured card: worst offender, 68% image, isolated in one variant, and it doesn't need the rest of the plan to be worth doing.
  • Four case studies are still stubs: welcome-to-my-city, sinomocene, teamchatviz, roche.

Shareable hook
I built my second case-study page on the system I'd designed for the first one — and the right call turned out to be not using it. The two pages share every primitive: spacing, type, media frames, buttons. What they don't share is the composition, because the designs genuinely differ. A design system earns its keep by making the shared parts unarguable, not by making every page the same shape.
